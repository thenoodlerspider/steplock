StepLock

Walk to unlock your phone. Literally.

Read this before you scroll past

Have you ever set a Screen Time limit, hit it, and then typed in your own password to get five more minutes?

Do you own a step tracker that has never once changed what you actually did that day?

Have you deleted an app because you were done with it, then put it back on your phone a week later?

If any of that sounds familiar, keep reading.

Why the usual apps don't work

Screen time blockers only work as long as you don't turn them off. And you're the one who set the limit, so you're also the one who can remove it. Most people do, within a couple weeks.

Step counters have the opposite problem. They just count. They never do anything with the number. Hitting 3,000 steps and hitting 10,000 steps feels the same to the app, so there's no real reason to push for more.

Nobody has tied these two things together. You get a tracker that watches you fail to move, and a blocker you can talk yourself out of. Neither one changes your day.

What StepLock does

It's simple. 100 steps equals 1 minute of screen time on the apps you choose to restrict.

You don't earn the time in advance and spend it whenever. The block is live. Open Instagram with zero minutes banked and you get a lock screen right there, not a warning, not a nag, an actual lock screen you have to get past by walking.

There's no password to bypass it because there's nothing to type. The only way in is steps.

The earning rate

The first steps of the day are worth more than the later ones.

0 to 1,000 steps: 1 minute per 100 steps
1,000 to 2,000 steps: 2 minutes per 100 steps
2,000 to 5,000 steps: 3 minutes per 100 steps, this is the peak
Past 5,000 steps: the rate drops to keep walking worth it without letting anyone bank unlimited screen time

Rough numbers so you know what to expect:

5,000 steps gets you about 2 hours
10,000 steps gets you about 3 hours
15,000 steps hits the daily cap at 4 hours

Walking more than that doesn't earn more. The cap exists on purpose. This isn't built so someone can grind out an entire evening of scrolling if they just walk enough.

Any minutes you don't use carry over to the next day, so missing your goal once doesn't wipe out what you've built up.

What else is in the app
You pick which apps get restricted. Nothing is locked unless you choose it.
A habit tracker, separate from steps, for anything else you're trying to build.
Streaks and achievements for steps, time of day walking, low screen time, and consistency.
A calendar view so you can look back at any day, not just today.
Uses Health Connect for step data, with a fallback to your phone's own step sensor if Health Connect isn't available.
A short notification when you hit your goal, unlock a new rate, or land an achievement. One line, nothing more.
Permissions

StepLock needs a few real permissions because it does something real. No account, no analytics, nothing leaves your phone.

Physical activity / Health Connect: reads your step count, that's all it's for
Usage access: checks how long your restricted apps have been used today
Accessibility service: detects when a restricted app opens so it can show the lock screen. It does not read what's on your screen, collect personal data, or interact with any app for you
Display over other apps: needed to show the lock screen over the app you opened, instead of just sending you back to your home screen
Notifications: for the low balance and milestone alerts

Every permission is explained in the app when it's requested, and you can revoke any of them later in your phone's settings.
